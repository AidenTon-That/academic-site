CATHODOLUMINESCENCE
CONCEPTUAL SCHEMATIC · NOT TO SCALE · NOT EXPERIMENTAL DATA
Cathodoluminescence

Reading the light
within a material.

When an electron beam excites a luminescent material, the emitted light can reveal information about crystal defects, composition and strain. This is the principle behind cathodoluminescence, one of the techniques identified in his UTS profile.

University of Strathclyde ↗ [7]
SYNCHROTRON X-RAY SPECTROSCOPY
CONCEPTUAL SCHEMATIC · NOT TO SCALE · NOT EXPERIMENTAL DATA
Synchrotron X-ray spectroscopy

Understanding what
surfaces are made of.

Synchrotron X-ray methods help investigate chemical and electronic properties. For example, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy examines electrons emitted from a material after X-ray excitation; ANSTO’s soft X-ray beamline supports this and related techniques.

ANSTO method guide ↗ [8]
ELECTRON MICROSCOPY
CONCEPTUAL SCHEMATIC · NOT TO SCALE · NOT EXPERIMENTAL DATA
Electron microscopy

Making the very small
visible.

Electron microscopy investigates material structure at very small scales. The resulting structural information helps researchers understand the relationship between a material’s structure and properties; beam damage is an important consideration for sensitive materials.

Microscopy review on PubMed ↗ [9]
02 / About the researcher

Curiosity, at
every scale.

Cuong Ton-ThatAssociate Professor · Faculty of Science

Cuong Ton-That is an Associate Professor at UTS, where his work focuses on oxide materials and nanostructures for optoelectronic and power electronic applications.

He earned a BSc in Physics with Honours and the University Medal at UTS, followed by a PhD in surface science at Robert Gordon University in the United Kingdom. Before joining UTS as a lecturer, he undertook postdoctoral research at the University of Cambridge’s Nanoscience Centre.

His UTS biography identifies industry research links with Nanovation and 3D-Oxides.
